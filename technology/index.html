Technology

From raw telemetry to grounded forecasts.

CAMEL's research bet: that ECG is a language-like substrate. Given enough long-horizon data, the right tokenization, and a curriculum that teaches a language model to cite its statistics, it becomes a forecaster, not just a classifier.

The clinical gap

A family of emergencies, managed only after they strike.

Heart-rhythm disorders and the arrhythmogenic conditions that trigger them span the full arc of clinical acuity. In the United States alone, sudden cardiac arrest, driven primarily by ventricular fibrillation and ventricular tachycardia, accounts for roughly 350,000 out-of-hospital and 292,000 in-hospital events each year, with survival rates remaining poor.1 Atrial fibrillation and atrial flutter, an order of magnitude more common, affect more than 52 million people worldwide and drive a disproportionate share of ischemic stroke.2 Obstructive sleep apnea, an arrhythmogenic risk factor rather than an arrhythmia itself, reaches nearly one billion adults globally.3

Despite this range in severity, these conditions share a clinical limitation: they are managed reactively, identified only after pathological markers have already manifested. For the most acute events, that delay is decisive: each minute without defibrillation lowers survival by 7–10%, and resuscitation becomes exceedingly unlikely past the ten-minute mark.4 For non-arrest arrhythmias and apneic episodes, the lost window is measured in hours of avoidable risk rather than seconds of mortality; the mechanism differs, but the shape of the failure does not.

The clinical value of prediction is therefore large and broadly distributed. Forecasting atrial fibrillation before it begins enables earlier rate control, such as timely beta-blockade instead of post-hoc cardioversion.5 Forecasting an apneic episode enables airway intervention before hypoxia cascades into arrhythmia. For acute arrest, every minute of lead time folds back into survival. CAMEL's target is to deliver that lead time, at a false-alarm rate the bedside can actually live with.

Why current AI falls short

Three gaps the field has quietly accepted.

01 · Data

Insufficient data.

Public ECG datasets are overwhelmingly 10-second, single-lead recordings, short enough to classify a rhythm but far too short to predict one. Training a forecasting model on ten-second windows is like training a language model on ten-token snippets.

02 · Accuracy

Brittle models, and hallucinations.

Supervised baselines posted a 31% specificity at 95% sensitivity in early 2025 — a 69% false-positive rate. Meanwhile, prompting general-purpose LLMs directly on raw ECG reliably produces plausible-sounding but unfounded readings: the worst of both worlds.

03 · Clinical fit

Designed around models, not workflows.

A model that earns a 0.92 AUROC on a research split but triggers once an hour in the ICU is not clinically deployable. Alarm fatigue, lead variability, and charting integration are first-class constraints — not after-the-fact wrapper concerns.

Our approach

Three pillars, each earned by the paper.

01

High-quality, long-horizon data

More than 1 billion 1-second segments across 13 public datasets, paired with a private 8-lead Penn ICU corpus spanning 10 minutes to 48 hours per patient and labeled for NORM, VT, and VF. This is not another 10-second benchmark.

Private corpus · 8-lead ICU · 10m–48h
02

Neurosymbolic, evidence-grounded reasoning

ECGDeli-computed statistics — RR intervals, PR, QRS duration, RMSSD, PAC counts — anchor every forecast. CAMEL's outputs cite specific beat-level metrics, making verification possible and hallucinations harder.

Grounding RMSE · 109 vs 304
03

Staged curriculum training

Five progressive stages — autoencoder, MC & short answer, statistics, multi-turn, forecasting — that teach the model to read a signal before it is asked to reason over one. Each stage has a measurable contribution in ablation.

5 stages · 1B → 17K samples

Architecture

How CAMEL reads a signal.

A trainable encoder quantizes the ECG; a lead-aware attention mask lets the model reason across leads at the same timestamp, while remaining causal for the text it generates.

Why this matters

Lead-aware attention enables variable lead counts, variable duration, and multi-ECG prompts — the three shapes a real ICU signal takes but prior encoders rejected.

Curriculum

Five stages, strictly in order.

Each stage has a distinct dataset, objective, and output model. We skip none and we do not parallelize.

01 · Autoencoder

Learn the signal.

1 B+ segments · MSE reconstruction.

02 · MC & short answer

Answer clinical Qs.

20 M Harvard-Emory samples.

03 · Statistics

Cite the numbers.

~30 M ECGDeli-grounded QA.

04 · Conversation

Multi-turn reasoning.

05 · Forecasting

Predict ahead.
